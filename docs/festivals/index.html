Festivals

Japanese Festivals Calendar

Plan your trip around Japan's famous seasons and celebrations --- or avoid the peaks --- with exact dates and crowd predictions for 2026.

How to Use This Calendar

How to Use

Red marks the extreme peaks — genius crowds, packed trains, and a real price surge. Amber means it's manageable with a little planning. Green is the calm, low-cost sweet spot. For empty temples and thin wallets, aim for mid-to-late June or throughout September.

8 Major Festivals & Seasons in 2026

Sapporo Snow Festival

High crowds

Early Feb 2026 * ~1 week

Hokkaido's centerpiece winter event: hundreds of giant ice and snow sculptures, huge illuminated displays, and snow slides along Odori Park.

Travel Tips

  • Bundle up — it's -10°C or below. Visit at night for the glow, and morning to dodge the biggest crowds.

Cherry Blossom (Sakura)

Extreme crowds

Late Mar - Early Apr In Tokyo * ~1-2 weeks

Japan's iconic season. Hanami picnics under blooming sakura in Ueno, Chidorigafuchi, and Kyoto's rivers. The 'sakura front' sweeps north from Kyushu to Hokkaido.

Travel Tips

  • Time it by tracking the official sakura forecast (published ~2 weeks ahead). Ueno and Philosopher's Path at 7 AM are bliss.

Golden Week

Extreme crowds

Apr 29 - May 5 2026 * 7 days

Four national holidays in one week — Japan's biggest travel period. Bullet trains sell out, hotels triple in price, and every attraction is packed.

Travel Tips

  • Avoid at all costs if you can.
  • Travel the week after (May 6-) for perfect spring weather with the crowds gone.

Gion Matsuri

KyotoHigh crowds

July 2026 * All month

One of Japan's three great festivals. The famous yamaboko floats parade through Kyoto on July 17, preceded by evening 'Yoiyama.'

Travel Tips

  • The floats parade twice — July 17 (front) and July 24 (after).
  • Book a hostel with a river view or spare the crowds at the Gion area.

Hanabi (Fireworks)

High crowds

* Evenings

Japan's summer obsession. Massive hanabi displays light up rivers and bays from Tokyo's Sumida River to Osaka's Yodo River — often with 500,000+ spectators.

Travel Tips

  • Reserve a riverside spot early or book a river cruise.
  • Check the local 'hanabi taikai' calendar to plan your route.

Obon Week

Extreme crowds

Mid August 2026 * 1 week

The Buddhist festival honoring ancestors. Families return to their hometowns — the second biggest travel rush of the year, alongside lantern-dropping ceremonies.

Travel Tips

  • Book everything 2-3 months ahead or travel in the days after Obon.
  • Witness a float lantern ceremony (Toronagashi) in Kyoto or Miyagi.

Aomori Nebuta Matsuri

Aomori CityHigh crowds

Aug 2-7 2026 * 6 days

Tohoku's loudest event: dozens of massive, painted paper battle floats lit from within, danced through Aomori's streets at night to drums and flutes.

Travel Tips

  • Catch the 'haneto' dancers — spectators are welcome to join.
  • Seats sell out early in summer; a street-side vantage is unforgettable.

Shichifukujin New Year

Moderate crowds

Late Dec - Early Jan * Week

Japan's New Year (Oshogatsu) — shrine visits, lucky decorations, and family feasts. By New Year's Eve, most shops and attractions close until Jan 3.

Travel Tips

  • Prices stay high but crowds thin after Jan 3.
  • See the first-sunrise (hatsu-hinode) at a coastal spot like Mt Takao.

Festival Travel Survival Tips

The Two 'Extreme' Peaks

Golden Week (late April-May 5) and Obon (mid-August) shut down everyday life. Trains sell out, hotels triple, prices surge. Avoid travel during these weeks, or book 4-6 months ahead.

Plan Around Seasons, Not In

The best strategy: arrive 5-7 days after sakura peak or after Golden Week. You get near-perfect weather with crowds 60-80% lower and hotels at shoulder-season prices.

Cultural Festivals Are Worth It

Gion Matsuri, Nebuta, and fireworks (hanabi) have manageable crowds compared to sakura and offer the deepest, most joyful Japanese experiences — the ones you'll talk about for years.
