Drinking Age in Japan: Is It 18 or 20? Rules for Tourists

Published September 2, 2026 · 5 min read · Updated October 14, 2026

The legal drinking age in Japan is 20. You must be at least 20 to drink alcohol, whether you live in Japan or are visiting on holiday. Being allowed to drink at 18 or 19 in your home country does not change the rule in Japan.

Japan lowered its age of adulthood to 18 in April 2022, which explains much of the confusion. The drinking age stayed at 20. The National Tax Agency’s current guidance explicitly confirms the distinction.

Is the drinking age in Japan 18 or 20?

It is 20, including for beer, wine, sake, shochu, whisky, and alcoholic canned drinks. A low alcohol percentage does not make a drink suitable for someone under 20.

The change to adulthood concerns civil matters such as entering contracts without parental consent. It did not lower every age restriction. The Ministry of Justice explains the adulthood reform, which took effect on April 1, 2022.

For a trip, the practical distinction is straightforward:

  • 18 or 19: being legally an adult does not let you drink alcohol in Japan.
  • 20 or older: you meet the drinking-age requirement, although a venue can have additional entry policies.
  • 21 or older: you do not need to wait until 21 under Japanese drinking-age rules.

Does the same drinking age apply to tourists?

Yes. There is no separate, lower drinking age for foreign visitors. A British or Australian visitor aged 18 cannot use their home country’s drinking age to order alcohol in Japan. An American visitor aged 20 meets Japan’s age requirement even though the rule at home is different.

The age rule also does not change between Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, and other destinations. What can differ is a venue’s admission policy or a local restriction on drinking in a particular public space.

Plan restaurant bookings and evening activities around the youngest person in your group. If a bar, club, or organized tour has its own minimum age, check that requirement before paying.

Can you buy alcohol in Japan at 18 or 19?

Do not buy alcohol for drinking if you are under 20. Japan’s rules prohibit businesses from knowingly selling or serving alcohol for consumption by someone under 20. Retailers and restaurants must take measures such as checking age to prevent under-20 drinking.

The National Tax Agency also tells retailers to display notices that they will not sell alcohol when they cannot confirm the customer is at least 20. That applies to ordinary purchases as well as an order at a bar: a supermarket or convenience store is not an exception.

Do not ask an older friend to purchase drinks for an under-20 member of your group. Choose a soft drink instead, and check what is actually in unfamiliar canned or bottled beverages.

Do you need ID to buy alcohol in Japan?

Be prepared to prove your age. Carry your original passport as a visitor and show it if staff ask; do not plan an evening around a photo or photocopy being accepted as age verification. The business decides whether it can verify your age from the document presented.

You may encounter an age-confirmation prompt at checkout, but pressing a button does not replace the age requirement or prevent staff from asking for ID. A purchase going through without a document check does not make underage drinking lawful.

For families, distinguish ordering food from being admitted to a venue. An izakaya serving meals and a late-night nightclub may have different entry policies. Ask the specific venue whether younger guests can enter rather than assuming every place follows the same rule.

Can parents give alcohol to someone under 20?

Parental permission does not create an exception to the under-20 drinking prohibition. The National Tax Agency’s explanation also states that parents or people acting in their place must stop drinking by minors under their supervision.

For a family dinner, order a separate non-alcoholic drink for anyone under 20. Do not assume that a small serving, a celebration, or drinking in a hotel room changes the age rule.

Is drinking in public allowed in Japan?

Meeting the drinking age does not mean you can drink everywhere. Tokyo’s official travel guide notes that public drinking can be seen in Japan but also identifies places that prohibit alcohol, including Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden. Follow the rules of the park, event, transport operator, or property you are using.

Shibuya is a particularly important example for visitors. Shibuya City’s ordinance information explains restrictions on drinking in designated public areas around Shibuya Station. Check the city’s current map and hours before your visit. Do not assume that buying a can nearby means you can drink it on the street.

For an easier evening, drink inside a venue where it is permitted, keep noise down, and put empty containers in the appropriate bin. For meal customs, see our Japan dining etiquette guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the legal drinking age in Japan in 2026?

It is 20. The National Tax Agency’s 2026 guidance confirms that the alcohol age restriction remains 20 even though civil adulthood begins at 18.

Can a 19-year-old tourist drink in Japan?

No. The same minimum age applies to visitors. A lower drinking age in your home country does not provide an exemption.

Can you drink sake at 18 in Japan?

No. Sake is an alcoholic beverage and is subject to the same drinking age as beer, wine, and spirits.

Is the drinking age different in Tokyo and Osaka?

No. The drinking-age requirement is national. Local public-space rules and individual venue entry policies can differ.

Did Japan lower its drinking age in 2022?

No. Japan lowered civil adulthood from 20 to 18 on April 1, 2022. The drinking age remained 20.
