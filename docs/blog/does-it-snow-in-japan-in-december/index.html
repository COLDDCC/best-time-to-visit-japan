Does It Snow in Japan in December? Where to Expect Snow

Published October 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Yes, snow falls in parts of Japan in December, especially northern and mountainous regions. But you should not plan a Tokyo or Osaka holiday around guaranteed snow. Location, elevation and the point in the month make a large difference.

Our Japan weather in December guide compares city temperatures and precipitation before you choose your route.

Where is snow more likely?

JNTO’s winter guidance emphasizes the large differences between regions. Hokkaido and snow-country destinations offer a different winter experience from Japan’s major Pacific-side cities.

Destination type How to plan a December trip
Hokkaido, including Sapporo Prepare for winter temperatures and possible snow or ice; check current local conditions.
Mountain resorts in Nagano and Niigata Check resort opening notices, accessible lifts and actual snow cover before booking around skiing.
Sea of Japan coast Expect a wetter winter climate; rain versus snow depends on the specific place and conditions.
Tokyo and Osaka Plan for cold city sightseeing, with snow as a possibility rather than the purpose of the trip.
Okinawa Plan a milder-weather visit rather than a snow holiday.

Does it snow in Tokyo in December?

Do not count on it. Tokyo’s average December high is 12.0°C and average low is 3.8°C. Those averages describe a relatively mild city winter compared with Sapporo, where the corresponding figures are 2.0°C and −4.0°C.

An average low above freezing does not prove snow is impossible. It simply does not support promising visitors a snowy Tokyo Christmas. If seeing snow is essential, choose a destination for that purpose and verify conditions near departure.

What about Osaka and Kyoto?

Osaka’s December average high is 12.3°C and low is 5.3°C. A normal Osaka sightseeing trip should not depend on snow. Kyoto has a separate local climate, and higher nearby terrain can differ from the city center; do not treat an occasional snowy temple photograph as a guarantee for your dates.

For a Kyoto or Osaka base, consider snow-country travel as a separate, condition-dependent excursion rather than an assumed feature of the city itinerary.

Early December versus late December

December is an early-season month for many ski areas. A planned opening date does not mean every lift and piste will operate, and later dates still cannot guarantee snow quality. Check the resort’s own snow report, open terrain, transport access and cancellation terms.

Avoid booking a long detour solely because a generic article says that all resorts open in December. Altitude and local weather matter, and conditions change year to year.

Is precipitation the same as snowfall?

No. JMA’s December precipitation normal is 114.5 mm in Sapporo and 225.9 mm in Niigata. Those are water-equivalent precipitation totals, not snow depth in centimeters. They cannot tell you how much snow is lying on the ground at a resort or whether a city street is icy.

Use local snow-depth reports and resort updates for decisions about skiing and winter excursions.

What should you pack for snow?

Bring insulated layers, a warm outer coat, gloves, a hat and waterproof footwear with appropriate grip. Snow-country footwear requirements differ from those for dry Tokyo pavements. Read our December clothing guide before combining the two in one trip.

Allow flexibility for winter transport disruption and check route-specific notices. Mountain roads, walking trails and city transit have different conditions; a clear forecast at your hotel is not enough to judge a high-elevation outing.

Quick questions

Can I see snow at Christmas? It depends on the destination and that year’s conditions. Check snow reports rather than treating the holiday date as a guarantee.

Is Hokkaido colder than Tokyo? Yes in the December station normals: Sapporo’s monthly mean is −0.9°C, compared with Tokyo’s 7.7°C.

Should I travel in November instead? Choose based on your intended activities. Read the November guide for cool-weather sightseeing and autumn travel.

Sources and how to use these figures

Weather figures use Japan Meteorological Agency climate normals for 1991–2020. They describe long-term conditions at individual stations, not the forecast for your holiday. Precipitation includes rain and melted snow; monthly totals do not tell you how many days will be wet. Check the JMA forecast near departure and again during your trip.
