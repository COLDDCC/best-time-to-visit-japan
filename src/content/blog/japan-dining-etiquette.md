Most "rules" you'll read online are minor. These two actually matter — both tied to funeral customs:

## The two chopstick rules
- **Never stand chopsticks upright in rice** — it echoes incense at a funeral altar.
- **Never pass food chopstick-to-chopstick** — it mirrors a bone-passing funeral ritual. Place food on the other person's plate instead.

Everything else (resting chopsticks wrong, fumbling with them) is genuinely fine.

## Say it before and after
"Itadakimasu" before eating, "gochisousama deshita" when done. Not required, but easy to pick up and appreciated.

## Slurping is normal
For ramen, soba, and udon — locals do it too. Don't feel self-conscious.

## Izakaya basics
- An automatic small appetizer (otoshi) is a cover charge, not something you ordered — a few hundred yen, normal.
- Order a few shared dishes at a time, not everything up front.
- "Sumimasen" gets a server's attention. No tipping.

## No tipping, anywhere
Good service is the standard. Extra cash can cause confusion — pay exactly what's billed.

## Splitting the bill
"Warikan" (splitting evenly) is common among friends, but smaller restaurants often prefer one payer at the register. Easiest in groups: designate one payer, settle up after.

## Bottom line
Get the chopstick rules right, watch the table next to you for the rest, and you're fine.
