**November is relatively dry in Tokyo and Osaka compared with September and October, but it is not dry everywhere in Japan.** The Sea of Japan coast can be much wetter. Pack for your destinations, rather than relying on one national rainfall average.

For temperatures, clothing and a broader trip overview, start with our [Japan weather in November guide](/month/november).

## Is November Japan’s rainy season?

November is outside the main early-summer rainy season, usually called tsuyu. That does not mean rain stops: weather systems can still bring wet days, while northern and mountain destinations may experience snow. [JNTO’s November guide](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/november/) describes generally crisp air and clear skies across much of Japan, alongside increasingly chilly mornings and evenings.

## How much rain does Japan get in November?

| City / station | October precipitation | November precipitation | December precipitation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tokyo | 234.8 mm | 96.3 mm | 57.9 mm |
| Osaka | 136.0 mm | 72.5 mm | 55.5 mm |
| Sapporo | 109.9 mm | 113.8 mm | 114.5 mm |
| Niigata | 157.7 mm | 203.5 mm | 225.9 mm |
| Naha, Okinawa | 179.2 mm | 119.1 mm | 110.0 mm |

These are 1991–2020 station normals. In cold places, some precipitation falls as snow. Sapporo’s figure is not a total of liquid rain alone, and Naha represents one location rather than every Okinawan island.

## Is November wetter than October or December?

For Tokyo and Osaka, November is drier than October by monthly precipitation, and December is drier again. Niigata follows a different pattern: its precipitation increases toward winter. That difference matters if your itinerary crosses from Tokyo to Japan’s Sea of Japan coast.

A lower monthly total does not guarantee that a particular seven-day visit will stay dry. You also cannot turn 96.3 mm into a personal probability of rain without information about daily rainfall and its distribution.

## Does it rain a lot in Tokyo or Kyoto in November?

Tokyo’s November normal is 96.3 mm, compared with 234.8 mm in October. That supports describing November as relatively dry, rather than rain-free. Keep one indoor option available for a wet afternoon.

Kyoto needs its own station record. Nearby Osaka offers regional context, but its 72.5 mm figure should not be presented as Kyoto’s rainfall. Kyoto’s temple walks also involve outdoor paths and steps, so wet-weather footwear matters even on an otherwise city-focused trip.

## Should you bring an umbrella or rain jacket?

A compact umbrella is useful for city sightseeing. A water-resistant jacket is practical on exposed paths, and comfortable shoes with grip help on wet pavement and stone steps. For a snow-country route, waterproof footwear and warm layers matter more than an umbrella alone.

Read our [November packing guide](/blog/what-to-wear-in-japan-in-november) for the differences between central Japan, Hokkaido and Okinawa.

## What if it rains during your trip?

Build a flexible itinerary instead of trying to predict every day months ahead. Keep a museum, indoor exhibition or shopping visit as an alternative, and swap it with an outdoor garden visit when the short-range forecast becomes clearer. Check opening times and any reservation rules before making the swap.

For hiking or mountain day trips, check local conditions as well as the city forecast. Persistent rain, wind, snow or ice may justify changing the route entirely.

## Quick questions

**Will it rain every day?** Monthly averages cannot answer that for your dates. November is not the main rainy season, but a wet spell is possible.

**Do I need waterproof shoes?** Comfortable shoes that tolerate some rain are helpful in cities. Choose more protective footwear for snow, slush or outdoor routes.

**Is December a better choice to avoid rain?** Tokyo and Osaka have lower December precipitation normals, but it is colder. Compare the [December weather guide](/month/december) before choosing solely on rainfall.

## Sources and how to use these figures

Weather figures use [Japan Meteorological Agency climate normals for 1991–2020](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/en/normal/normal.html). They describe long-term conditions at individual stations, not the forecast for your holiday. Precipitation includes rain and melted snow; monthly totals do not tell you how many days will be wet. Check the [JMA forecast](https://www.jma.go.jp/bosai/forecast/) near departure and again during your trip.
