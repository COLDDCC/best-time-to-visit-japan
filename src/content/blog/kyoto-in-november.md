For the wider route and month choice, start with [Japan in November](/month/november/). The station table above uses Kyoto's own measurements, rather than substituting Osaka.

## Kyoto weather and the morning-to-evening difference

The average daily high and low in the station table describe typical daytime and overnight conditions across the full month. They are not temperatures you will necessarily encounter on one day. Carry a removable sweater and jacket so you can adjust between outdoor walks, transport and heated interiors.

For an evening outdoors, check the hourly forecast and bring the warmer layer with you. A mountain excursion needs its own forecast and route conditions; the Kyoto urban station is not a mountain weather report.

## Autumn leaves: choose places with current reports

Use [Kyoto City's official foliage reports](https://ja.kyoto.travel/flower/momiji/) to compare individual locations. Check the report date and year: an older calendar or photograph does not establish current conditions. Higher ground and city gardens can be at different stages on the same day.

Keep the order of garden visits flexible within your stay. Confirm current opening hours, reservation requirements and any evening admission arrangements on the venue's own website. This guide does not predict a peak date or queue duration.

## Temple paths, shoes and socks

Choose broken-in walking shoes with grip for wet stone, steps and garden paths. Some indoor temple areas ask visitors to remove shoes; follow the posted instructions and wear clean socks. Easy-to-remove footwear can make repeated transitions simpler, but walking comfort comes first.

A compact umbrella is a practical city starting point. Waterproof footwear is a route and forecast decision rather than a requirement for every Kyoto November visit. ## A flexible Kyoto rainy-day plan

| Planned outdoor time | Backup to keep available | Check before changing plans |
|---|---|---|
| Garden walk | Museum or indoor exhibition | Opening days and reservation rules |
| Temple grounds | A permitted indoor cultural visit | Which areas are sheltered and where shoes come off |
| Long neighborhood walk | Shorter walks between indoor stops | Local forecast and walking surfaces |

These are planning options, not promises that an attraction will be open. Keep the backup close to your accommodation or planned area to reduce unnecessary transfers in rain. Read [November rainy days by city](/blog/is-japan-rainy-in-november/) when comparing Kyoto with other destinations.

## Crowds and choosing a base

Check the [Japan crowd calendar](/crowd-calendar/) for holiday context and verify local event dates. Compare accommodation for your exact stay. Choose Kyoto or Osaka based on the places you plan to visit, transport connections and availability; neither a fixed hotel saving nor a fixed temple queue is assumed here.

For trips continuing into winter, compare [late November and early December](/blog/japan-weather-late-november-early-december/). For a different city, use [Tokyo weather in November](/blog/tokyo-in-november-weather/).

## Sources and limits

All measurements in the shared station table link directly to the JMA station normals. Foliage planning uses Kyoto City's official reports. Climate averages help with preparation; use the [JMA forecast](https://www.jma.go.jp/bosai/forecast/) for your actual travel dates.
